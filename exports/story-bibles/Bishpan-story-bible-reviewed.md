**Problems still open:**
- **[blocker] Story doctor — Episode 3, 4, & 5 (The locked storage room):** Sulochana fled with the rusted key 15 years ago, and Soumya uses it to unlock the 'forgotten' temple storage room in Episode 4. Yet, Madhab supposedly hid the killer's recently bloody clothes in this exact same room. If the room was locked for 15 years, Madhab could not have accessed it. Furthermore, it makes no logical sense for Balaram or Deba to leave the empty Hira Akhi velvet box inside the temple storage room after stealing the diamonds. *Fix:* Make the room itself generally accessible (but abandoned), allowing Madhab to hide clothes there. The rusted key from Sulochana should instead open a specific locked chest or floor safe hidden beneath the debris in that room, where the Pujari had secured the empty velvet box as evidence before he was murdered.
- **[major] Story doctor — Episodes 1 & 3 (The Mystery Giveaway):** The user explicitly asked that the audience not know it is the hero until later. However, in Episode 1, Soumya discovers painfully bruised knuckles immediately after a violent murder, and in Episode 3, a scalpel goes missing from his bag. These clues are far too heavy-handed; viewers will immediately guess the sleepwalking doctor is the killer. *Fix:* Remove the bruised knuckles in Episode 1. For the missing scalpel in Episode 3, provide a concrete red herring: show Madhab explicitly stealing it and later attempting to use it for an amateur medical procedure on a villager, so the audience genuinely believes Madhab is the one utilizing the stolen medical tools for murder.
- **[major] Story doctor — Episode 2 (The Surveyor's Death):** The rules explicitly state the Goddess 'commandeers Soumya's medical genius, using real-world chemicals, scalpels, and anatomy'. Sabotaging a car's AC intake to pump red dust and jamming car doors is mechanical engineering, not medical science. It also strains plausibility that the surveyor couldn't just break a window or turn off the AC. *Fix:* Align the kill with Soumya's medical skills. Have the sleepwalking Soumya break into the surveyor's home and replace the medication in his asthma inhaler with a lethal, caustic powder (e.g., crushed toxic chemicals mimicking red dirt). When the surveyor uses it, his airways paralyze.
- **[major] Story doctor — Episode 8 (The Climax Sedation):** Soumya is surrounded by a mob and actively being beaten to his knees. Snatching a syringe and injecting a psychiatric sedative wouldn't instantly render him deeply asleep, nor would his heavily sedated body logically be able to jump up and catch a stick mid-air a second later without violating the 'no magic' rule. *Fix:* Change the timing. Soumya realizes the mob is about to breach the temple. Barricaded inside, he prepares and injects a fast-acting anesthetic (like Propofol) into his own IV line. By the time the mob smashes the doors and drags his limp body out, his conscious mind is already fully suppressed, allowing the Goddess to fluidly take over before Balaram strikes.
- **[blocker] Audience critic — Episodes 1-6 Pacing and Mystery:** The audience will guess Soumya is the killer by Episode 2 (murders require medical precision, he has bruised knuckles, he suffers from extreme exhaustion). Waiting until Episode 6 for the CCTV reveal makes the hero look incompetent and the audience incredibly bored. *Fix:* Move the CCTV reveal to Episode 3. Shift the genre for the second half from a whodunit to a tense thriller where Soumya desperately tries to chain himself to his bed at night while covering his tracks by day.
- **[major] Audience critic — Episode 4 and 5 Logic:** In Episode 4, Soumya uses Sulochana's key (brought from the city) to open the forgotten storage room. In Episode 5, Madhab claims he hid the bloody clothes inside it. How did Madhab get into a locked room if Soumya had the only key in Bhubaneswar? *Fix:* Sulochana's key should open a specific locked antique chest or a false bottom beneath the idol where the empty Hira Akhi box is kept, while Madhab simply hides the clothes in the generally accessible temple shadows.
- **[major] Audience critic — Episode 5 (Kavita's arc):** Kavita drops into the story in Episode 5 purely to deliver a sedative and act as a plot device. We have no reason to care about their relationship because we haven't seen them together. *Fix:* Kavita must travel to Rambhadipur with Soumya in Episode 1 to co-run the medical camp. Her constant presence makes his attempts to hide his exhaustion harder and her eventual discovery more emotionally devastating.
- **[major] Odisha culture expert — Episode 8 (The Climax):** Using a psychiatric sedative to intentionally 'summon' Goddess Kali. In Odia rural culture, reducing divine possession (Bhar/Kalasi) to a drug's side-effect might offend devotees and undercuts the spiritual awe of the moment. The Goddess shouldn't rely on a clinical syringe to take control of her chosen vessel at the peak of her own festival. *Fix:* Have Kavita attempt to inject Soumya to *stop* his condition and save him from the mob. Soumya stops her, throws the syringe away, and instead consciously surrenders to the hypnotic, rhythmic Dhol (drums) of the Meru Jatra. This merges his medical condition with the traditional Odia method of inducing divine trance (Bhar).

## 1. The promise

**Logline:** When a mysterious madness strikes the village that exiled his family, a pragmatic doctor returns to cure the afflicted, only to discover the disease is a bloody retribution acting through his own sleepwalking body.

**The question that keeps people watching:** Is the village suffering from a toxic epidemic, or is the Goddess actually exacting a long-delayed blood debt through an invisible assassin?

**What the audience is paying for:** Folk-horror and psychological dread set against the intense, visceral backdrop of Odisha's rural penance rituals.

**Why "wow":** The horrific realization that the hero's supposed medical condition of sleepwalking is actually the Goddess commandeering his brilliant medical skills to enact brutal, ritualistic justice.

## 2. The hidden truth

Fifteen years ago, ambitious local politician Balaram Rout orchestrated the theft of the Kali idol's antique Zamindar-endowed gold-and-diamond eyes (Hira Akhi) to fund his first election campaign. He used local contractor Deba to physically steal them, hastily replacing them with cheap brass eyes so the idol wouldn't be declared Khandita (desecrated), though the temple lost its glory. When the temple Pujari discovered the theft, Balaram and Deba framed him, whipping the village into a frenzy during the Chaitra month festival. The mob brutally murdered the Pujari. His mother, Sulochana, fled to Bhubaneswar with her young grandson, Soumya. Sulochana knew the truth but hid it in mythical stories about Danda Nata penances. Today, following Sulochana's death, the trauma has awakened within Soumya, allowing the Goddess Kali to possess his unconscious, sleepwalking body. Utilizing his profound medical knowledge, she enacts ritualistic vengeance on the guilty by staging impossible supernatural deaths using chemicals and anatomy.

- **April 2009:** Balaram Rout and Deba steal the Kali idol's antique Hira Akhi (diamond eyes) from the Rambhadipur temple, replacing them with cheap brass eyes.
- **April 2009 (Next Day):** The Pujari discovers the theft. Balaram incites a mob, accusing the Pujari of the crime. The Pujari is beaten to death.
- **April 2009 (Night):** Sulochana escapes Rambhadipur with young Soumya, relocating to Bhubaneswar and changing their last name.
- **2010 to 2023:** Balaram becomes a powerful MLA. Soumya grows up to become a successful doctor, suppressing his childhood memories and developing severe parasomnia.
- **March 2024:** Sulochana passes away, leaving behind cassette tapes of her stories. The grief triggers Soumya's sleepwalking, and the Goddess takes hold.
- **April 2024 (Start of Series):** Soumya returns to Rambhadipur to run a free health camp, unaware that at night he is hunting down his father's killers.

**Who knows what at the start:**
- **Dr. Soumya Dash:** Thinks his father died in a random communal riot and believes his grandmother's stories were just folklore. He hides his severe parasomnia from his colleagues.
- **Balaram Rout:** Knows he orchestrated the theft and murder, and hides the stolen diamond eyes inside his signature silver walking stick.
- **Deba:** Knows he physically stole the eyes and struck the first blow against the Pujari.
- **Inspector Meera:** Knows the contractor's death was not natural, hiding her suspicion that the old temple faction is involved.
- **Madhab:** Knows someone is leaving bloody clothes in the Kali temple at night, but hides them to protect the temple's sanctity.

## 3. Characters

### Dr. Soumya Dash (28) — Hero
*Protagonist / Unwitting Assassin. Clean-cut city doctor, wears scrubs or neat casuals, always looks slightly exhausted with dark circles.*

- **Wants:** To cure the strange epidemic in his ancestral village and honor his late grandmother's memory.
- **Needs:** To uncover the horrific truth of his father's death and accept his dark blood-legacy.
- **Secret:** He suffers from severe, violent sleepwalking episodes (parasomnia) that he hides from the world.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes science and medicine can explain every human affliction and that the past is firmly dead.
- **Changes the plot when:** He discovers his own guilt by secretly installing a CCTV camera in his clinic to monitor his night-terrors, only to watch footage of himself leaving with a surgical scalpel.
- **Arc:** From a pragmatic, rational man of science to a fugitive avatar of divine retribution who sacrifices his normal life for justice.

### Balaram Rout (55) — Antagonist (The man who framed the hero's father)
*Villain. Crisp white kurtas, heavy gold rings, walks with a silver-headed cane, always accompanied by armed guards.*

- **Wants:** To win the upcoming election and bulldoze the old Kali temple to build a lucrative tourist resort.
- **Needs:** To be held accountable for the blood on his hands.
- **Secret:** He possesses the original Hira Akhi (diamond eyes) of the Goddess, hiding them inside his silver walking stick.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes power and money make him immune to consequences and that religion is just a tool to control the masses.
- **Changes the plot when:** He frames Soumya for the new murders, planting evidence to recreate the exact mob justice he used on Soumya's father.
- **Arc:** From an untouchable, arrogant ruler of the region to a terrified, broken man executed publicly during the final ritual.

### Kavita (27) — His fiancee
*Ally / Foil. Modern, practical, wears elegant sarees or doctor's coats, carries high-end medical gear.*

- **Wants:** To bring Soumya back to Bhubaneswar and marry him.
- **Needs:** To realize she cannot medically save Soumya from his supernatural destiny.
- **Secret:** She realizes Soumya is the killer before he does, and tries to sedate him to hide his guilt from the law.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes love and chemical intervention can fix any deep-rooted psychological trauma.
- **Changes the plot when:** She helps Meera uncover the medical anomalies in the autopsies, then tries to forcefully sedate Soumya to hide his guilt, inadvertently trapping him in the village when the mob arrives.
- **Arc:** From a practical medical partner trying to fix her fiance to a horrified witness who must let the man she loves go.

### Inspector Meera (30) — No relation - the investigating police officer
*Skeptic / Detective. Tough, practical police uniform, carries a notebook and a heavy flashlight.*

- **Wants:** To logically solve the bizarre murders and get a promotion to the city.
- **Needs:** To accept that some forces in Rambhadipur defy the Indian Penal Code.
- **Secret:** She was demoted to this village for speaking out against a corrupt politician and hates politicians.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes every crime has a clear, mundane motive and a rational human perpetrator.
- **Changes the plot when:** She insists on a thorough secondary autopsy in the city, uncovering the hidden chemical and surgical evidence that proves a brilliant doctor is the killer.
- **Arc:** From a strict enforcer of the law to someone who looks the other way, allowing the Goddess's justice to be completed.

### Deba (45) — No relation - the wealthy local contractor
*Henchman / Victim. Loud printed shirts, heavy gold chains, constantly chewing paan.*

- **Wants:** To secure the temple demolition contract from Balaram.
- **Needs:** To confess his sins before the Goddess.
- **Secret:** He kept a silver coin from the temple treasury as a twisted lucky charm.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes his wealth has washed away the sins of his past.
- **Changes the plot when:** He wanders into a dark alley during the festival and is ambushed by Soumya, dying in the cold open and setting the investigation in motion.
- **Arc:** Dies early, establishing the terrifying stakes and the method of the Goddess's vengeance.

### Sulochana (75) — His father's mother (deceased)
*Mentor (Posthumous). Seen in flashbacks and photos: traditional white widow's saree, deep wrinkles, fearful eyes.*

- **Wants:** To protect Soumya from the village's curse.
- **Needs:** To tell the truth about her son's murder.
- **Secret:** She recognized Deba and Balaram in the mob but stayed silent to keep Soumya alive.
- **Lie they believe:** Believed running away would permanently sever their ties to Kali Mata.
- **Changes the plot when:** Leaves behind audio cassettes disguising the perpetrators' identities as demons in Danda Nata fables, which Soumya decodes.
- **Arc:** Remains a tragic figure, but her voice from the past guides Soumya to the truth.

### Madhab (25) — No relation - local temple caretaker
*False Suspect. Ash smeared on forehead, wears simple dhoti, gaunt and intense.*

- **Wants:** To restore the Kali temple to its former glory.
- **Needs:** To find a true purpose beyond blind fanaticism.
- **Secret:** He has been sneaking into the clinic at night to steal medical supplies for the village poor.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes the Goddess requires random human blood sacrifice from the villagers to be appeased.
- **Changes the plot when:** He finds the killer's bloody clothes in the temple and hides them in the locked storage room to protect the temple's sanctity, inadvertently making himself look like the killer.
- **Arc:** From a creepy, suspicious fanatic to a loyal devotee who realizes the true avatar is Soumya.

**The villain's plan:** Balaram Rout plans to secure his upcoming re-election by initiating a massive commercial development over the sacred temple lands, turning Rambhadipur into a lucrative tourist hub. He plans to bulldoze the Kali temple entirely using a fabricated clearance document from the Commissioner of Endowments, Odisha. When the bizarre murders of his old co-conspirators begin, he realizes someone is targeting his past. Believing it to be a surviving relative or political sabotage, his plan is to identify the killer and frame them for the crimes, inciting a village mob to execute them—exactly as he did to the Pujari 15 years ago. He is completely convinced he is right, believing that progress, wealth, and power justify erasing obsolete, superstitious traditions and anyone who clings to them.

## 4. Facts sheet

**Jobs and money:**
- Dr. Soumya earns a lucrative salary at a private city hospital but carries a heavy mortgage, leaving him with little liquid cash.
- Balaram Rout possesses massive untraceable cash reserves hidden in agricultural fronts, allowing him to buy off local authorities.
- Rambhadipur's economy is entirely dependent on Balaram's construction jobs and seasonal farming, making the villagers fearful of crossing him.
- The local clinic is severely underfunded, relying on expired medicines and donations, prompting Soumya to fund it out of pocket.

**Health:**
- Soumya suffers from severe clinical parasomnia (sleepwalking) triggered by extreme stress; his body moves fluidly while his mind is completely unconscious.
- The victims die from mathematically precise, medically induced trauma mimicking Danda Nata penances (e.g., paralytic laryngospasm mimicking drowning, caustic chemicals mimicking internal fire).
- Sulochana died of a sudden, severe stroke, which acted as the psychological trigger for Soumya's repressed memories.
- Due to his medical training, Soumya has high physical stamina and anatomical knowledge, which the Goddess utilizes to enact precise, lethal justice without using magic.

**Belongings and vehicles:**
- Soumya drives a mid-range, heavily financed SUV that stands out awkwardly in the dusty village streets.
- Balaram travels in a convoy of matching white Scorpios, symbolizing his political dominance and intimidation.
- Sulochana left behind a locked wooden box containing old cassette tapes and a rusted brass key to the temple's forgotten storage room.
- The local clinic lacks a working ambulance, forcing characters to rely on personal vehicles for emergency transport.

**Places:**
- Rambhadipur, located in the Ganjam district, is a sweltering, drought-prone village characterized by deep red dust, intense heat, and a deeply rooted Danda Nata tradition.
- The old Kali temple is half-ruined, overgrown with banyan roots, housing an idol with cheap brass eyes replacing the stolen diamonds.
- The village clinic is a dusty, poorly lit two-room concrete structure with flickering tube lights and a rusty examination table.
- Soumya's apartment in Bhubaneswar is sleek, sterile, and heavily air-conditioned, representing the safe, modern world he is forced to abandon.

**Rules of this world:**
- The Goddess cannot manifest as a ghost or floating spirit; she strictly requires a traumatized human blood-vessel from the Pujari's lineage to affect the physical world.
- The possession can only occur when the vessel's conscious mind is completely suppressed, which happens during Soumya's deep sleepwalking states.
- The Goddess does not use magic to kill; she commandeers Soumya's medical genius, using real-world chemicals, scalpels, and anatomy to stage supernatural-looking Danda Nata penances.
- Technology functions normally in this world; the supernatural does not disable cameras or phones, which is why Soumya's actions are ultimately captured on CCTV.

## 5. The climax (designed first)

**Where/when:** The final night of the Chaitra month festival during the Meru Jatra, directly in front of the half-ruined Kali temple in Rambhadipur, surrounded by a violent village mob.

**The reversal:** Balaram successfully incites the mob to execute Soumya, perfectly mirroring the Pujari's murder 15 years ago. Instead of trying to escape or reason with them, Soumya realizes the only way to expose Balaram and save the village is to stop fighting his condition and let history repeat itself with a divine twist.

**The hero's choice:** Soumya deliberately injects himself with Kavita's heavy psychiatric sedative, intentionally inducing the deep-sleep state required for the Goddess to possess him completely, knowing he will wake up a murderer.

**The cost:** He permanently breaks his medical oath, becomes a wanted fugitive on the run, loses his modern life and Kavita, and accepts his fate as the eternal, unwilling vessel of Kali Mata.

**Payoffs that fire:**
- Kavita's psychiatric sedative syringe.
- Sulochana's cassette tape fable about the Demon King's silver leg.
- The Danda Nata Jhuna (flammable resin) throwing ritual.

**Final image:** Soumya, now a wanted fugitive, walking alone on a dark Odia highway at dawn. He walks with a slight, rhythmic limp, and the unmistakable sound of Danda Nata ankle bells syncs perfectly with his footsteps in the otherwise silent morning.

**Why "wow":** The audience realizes the hero's ultimate victory requires him to actively surrender his free will to the Goddess, weaponizing a debilitating medical condition to enact divine justice right in front of the villain who thought he had won.

**After:** Balaram is dead and his 15-year corruption is exposed. Inspector Meera closes the old murder file but must now hunt Soumya. Kavita returns to the city heartbroken. The village's Kali idol finally has its antique Hira Akhi (diamond eyes) restored by the blood-soaked hands of the Pujari's son.

## 7. Episode blueprints

### Episode 1: The Eyeless Mother

- **Cold open:** The sweltering heat of Chaitra month in Ganjam. Danda Nata dancers perform on burning coals, throwing Jhuna resin. Wealthy contractor Deba wanders into a dark alley near the temple ruins to urinate. An unseen force violently ambushes him in the shadows. He staggers back into the firelight, screaming in agony as his throat burns from the inside out, and collapses dead.
- **Question:** What invisible force is causing a grotesque, fiery epidemic in the village of Rambhadipur?
- **Hero's move:** Dr. Soumya pretends to check Deba's airway in the clinic, secretly using forceps to extract a blackened, ancient silver coin from Deba's throat just before Balaram's men seize the body.
- **Villain's move:** MLA Balaram Rout orders his men to seize Deba's body and cremate it without a police report, desperate to destroy any evidence connecting the death to the temple.
- **Clue (earned):** Soumya matches the silver coin's insignia to a dusty temple registry he finds abandoned in the clinic's storage, realizing the victim was stealing.
- **False lead:** The village headman claims Deba contracted a rare, highly contagious skin virus from the city.
- **Hero learns:** The physical trauma on the victim isn't a virus; someone poured a highly caustic clinical chemical down his throat to mimic internal fire.
- **Audience learns:** Balaram is terrified of something from his past, and Soumya is deeply connected to this temple.
- **Set piece:** The chaotic, hypnotic Danda Nata Agni Danda (fire penance) ritual, filled with drums, trance, and sudden, inexplicable death.
- **Side story:** Inspector Meera arrives from the city, instantly suspicious of Balaram's rushed cremation orders.
- **Turn:** Soumya decides to stay in Rambhadipur, using his own money to keep the clinic open to investigate the 'disease' himself.
- **Ending hook:** Soumya vigorously washes Deba's blood from his hands at the clinic basin, but as he dries them, he discovers his knuckles are deeply, painfully bruised. He dismisses it as an injury from moving clinic equipment.
- **Plants:** Danda Nata performers throwing Jhuna (flammable tree resin) into fire.; Sulochana's rusted brass temple key.

### Episode 2: Demon of the Red Dirt

- **Cold open:** Soumya drives his heavily financed SUV through the claustrophobic, red-dust lanes of Rambhadipur, listening to his late grandmother's cassette tapes. He blows a tire right outside the overgrown ruins of the Kali temple, whose idol has cheap brass eyes.
- **Question:** Who is targeting Balaram's inner circle using ancient folk methods?
- **Hero's move:** Soumya decodes his grandmother's taped fable about the 'Demon of the Red Dirt' and cross-references it with local land deeds, tracking down Balaram's corrupt former surveyor.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram pressures the local Tehsildar, producing a fabricated clearance document from the Commissioner of Endowments, Odisha, to legally bulldoze the temple.
- **Clue (earned):** Soumya breaks into the surveyor's locked field office and finds a hidden ledger detailing illegal temple land transfers from 15 years ago.
- **False lead:** The surveyor's death looks like a severe asthma attack brought on by a sudden Ganjam dust storm.
- **Hero learns:** The surveyor and Deba were both part of a specific land-grab operation fifteen years ago.
- **Audience learns:** The 'Demon' in Sulochana's fairy tale is actually Balaram Rout.
- **Set piece:** The Dhuli Danda (Dust Penance) sequence: The corrupt surveyor is trapped in his own vehicle, unable to open the doors, as fine red dust is forcibly dumped into the AC intake, suffocating him.
- **Side story:** Balaram walks with a prominent silver-headed walking stick, aggressively using it to point and intimidate the villagers.
- **Turn:** Inspector Meera confiscates the ledger from Soumya, warning him not to play detective.
- **Ending hook:** Soumya searches a stolen medical supply bag recovered from outside his clinic and finds the dead surveyor's dropped pocket watch—heavy evidence pointing directly to the clinic thief.
- **Plants:** Sulochana's audio cassette about the silver leg.

### Episode 3: The Midnight Thief

- **Cold open:** Soumya wakes up on his clinic cot, exhausted, sweating, and suffering from a severe migraine. He checks his medical kit and finds a surgical scalpel missing.
- **Question:** Is the eccentric temple caretaker executing Balaram's men?
- **Hero's move:** Refusing to involve the corrupt local police, Soumya conducts a midnight stakeout in the pitch-black clinic to catch the intruder who has been stealing supplies and tools.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram feeds Inspector Meera a fabricated tip that the temple caretaker, Madhab, has a violent grudge against the dead men.
- **Clue (earned):** Soumya catches Madhab sneaking in, but discovers Madhab is only stealing expired painkillers to treat poor villagers Balaram banned from the hospital.
- **False lead:** Madhab is heavily framed as the killer by the police due to the surveyor's watch and his late-night activities in the clinic.
- **Hero learns:** Madhab's motives for stealing are purely altruistic, meaning he is likely not a calculated murderer, though he did take the scalpel for medical use.
- **Audience learns:** Inspector Meera is closing in on making an arrest that suits Balaram's narrative perfectly.
- **Set piece:** A tense, silent nighttime stakeout inside the clinic, as Soumya waits with a flashlight for the thief to arrive.
- **Side story:** Meera builds a circumstantial case against Madhab based on the planted evidence and his temple fanaticism.
- **Turn:** Inspector Meera arrests Madhab based on Balaram's tip, closing the official investigation.
- **Ending hook:** That night, Soumya sees a shadow sneaking away from the supposedly abandoned, locked Kali temple storage room, making him realize the true answers are hidden inside.
- **Plants:** The abandoned Kali temple storage room.

### Episode 4: The Water Penance

- **Cold open:** Flashback to April 2009: A young Soumya cowers under a bed as violent mob noises echo outside. His grandmother Sulochana frantically packs a single bag, her hands covered in someone else's blood.
- **Question:** What is the true connection between Soumya's family and the targeted men?
- **Hero's move:** Soumya uses the rusted brass key from his grandmother's belongings to unlock the forbidden temple storage room, searching for what was being hidden there.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram, fearing the killer is closing in, uses his political power to order the corrupt local police to execute a brutal, unwarranted raid on Soumya's clinic under the guise of an inspection, destroying equipment.
- **Clue (earned):** Inside the storage room, Soumya finds generic bloody clothes and the victims' stolen belongings hidden next to an empty velvet jewelry box—the Hira Akhi box.
- **False lead:** The local drunk claims he saw a vengeful ghost drowning people in the pond.
- **Hero learns:** The Kali idol did not just lose its eyes to decay; they were violently pried out and stolen, and the current brass eyes are a desecrating cover-up.
- **Audience learns:** Inspector Meera discovers a heavily redacted 15-year-old case file revealing Soumya's true last name and his identity as the murdered Pujari's son.
- **Set piece:** The Pani Danda (Water Penance): Balaram's former IIC (Inspector In-Charge) is found standing frozen in the waist-deep village pond, dead. He drowned with completely dry lungs.
- **Side story:** The local village doctor performs a basic autopsy on the IIC and completely misses the microscopic medical evidence.
- **Turn:** Meera confronts Soumya at the clinic, slamming the 15-year-old case file on his desk, exposing his hidden identity.
- **Ending hook:** Balaram silently unlocks his secret vault, stares at the blinding diamond eyes, and plots to incite the village against Soumya.
- **Plants:** Hidden medical evidence on the drowned IIC.
- **Pays off:** Sulochana's rusted brass key opens the storage room.

### Episode 5: Red Dust

- **Cold open:** Kavita arrives in Rambhadipur in a sleek, air-conditioned city car. She steps out into the sweltering, dusty village and is horrified to find Soumya looking manic, hollow-eyed, and cleaning up his trashed clinic after the police raid.
- **Question:** Can Soumya's modern medical logic explain away his deteriorating mental state?
- **Hero's move:** Prompted by Kavita pointing out his filthy, red-dust-covered feet (a sign he's been walking outside at night), Soumya purchases and secretly installs a battery-operated CCTV camera to monitor his own bed while he sleeps.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram spreads a rumor that the 'demon' who killed the Pujari has returned as a city doctor, laying the groundwork for mob justice.
- **Clue (earned):** Madhab (from jail) tells Soumya he found the bloody clothes draped over the Kali idol and hid them in the storage to protect the temple's sanctity, confirming they didn't magically appear.
- **False lead:** Kavita insists Soumya's symptoms are just extreme stress-induced psychosis from being back in a traumatizing place.
- **Hero learns:** Madhab is completely innocent, and whoever is committing the murders has intimate access to the clinic.
- **Audience learns:** Kavita secretly starts helping Meera review the autopsy files, noticing anomalies the local doctor missed.
