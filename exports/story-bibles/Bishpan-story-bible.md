# Bishpan: The Dust Penance — Story Bible

*a web series of 8 episodes, 20 minutes each*

## 1. The promise

**Logline:** When a mysterious madness strikes the village that exiled his family, a pragmatic doctor returns to cure the afflicted, only to discover the disease is a bloody retribution acting through his own hands.

**The question that keeps people watching:** Is the village suffering from a toxic epidemic, or is the Goddess actually exacting a long-delayed blood debt through an invisible assassin?

**What the audience is paying for:** Folk-horror and psychological dread set against the intense, visceral backdrop of Odisha's rural penance rituals.

**Why "wow":** The horrific realization that the hero's supposed medical condition of sleepwalking is actually the Goddess commandeering his body for brutal, ritualistic justice.

## 2. The hidden truth

Fifteen years ago, ambitious local politician Balaram Rout orchestrated the theft of the Kali idol's priceless diamond eyes to fund his first election campaign. He used local contractor Deba to physically steal them. When the temple Pujari discovered the theft, Balaram and Deba framed him, whipping the village into a frenzy during the Chaitra month festival. The mob brutally murdered the Pujari. His mother, Sulochana, fled to Bhubaneswar with her young grandson, Soumya. Sulochana knew the truth but was too terrified to speak out, instead hiding the facts inside mythical stories about the Danda Nata penances. Today, following Sulochana's death, the trauma has awakened within Soumya, allowing the Goddess Kali to possess his unconscious, sleepwalking body to enact ritualistic vengeance.

- **April 2009:** Balaram Rout and Deba steal the Kali idol's eyes from the Rambhadipur temple.
- **April 2009 (Next Day):** The Pujari discovers the theft. Balaram incites a mob, accusing the Pujari of the crime. The Pujari is beaten to death.
- **April 2009 (Night):** Sulochana escapes Rambhadipur with young Soumya, relocating to Bhubaneswar and changing their last name.
- **2010 to 2023:** Balaram becomes a powerful MLA. Soumya grows up to become a successful doctor, suppressing his childhood memories and developing a parasomnia condition.
- **March 2024:** Sulochana passes away, leaving behind cassette tapes of her stories. The grief triggers Soumya's sleepwalking, and the Goddess takes hold.
- **April 2024 (Start of Series):** Soumya returns to Rambhadipur to run a free health camp, unaware that at night he is hunting down his father's killers.

**Who knows what at the start:**

- **Dr. Soumya Dash:** Thinks his father died in a random communal riot and believes his grandmother's stories were just folklore. He hides his severe sleepwalking condition from his colleagues.
- **Balaram Rout:** Knows he orchestrated the theft and murder, and hides the stolen diamond eyes in a secret vault.
- **Deba:** Knows he physically stole the eyes and struck the first blow against the Pujari.
- **Inspector Meera:** Knows the contractor's death was not natural, hiding her suspicion that the old temple faction is involved.

## 3. Characters

### Dr. Soumya Dash (28) — The Hero (The Pujari's son)
*Protagonist / Unwitting Assassin. Clean-cut city doctor, wears scrubs or neat casuals, always looks slightly exhausted with dark circles.*

- **Wants:** To cure the strange epidemic in his ancestral village and honor his late grandmother's memory.
- **Needs:** To uncover the horrific truth of his father's death and accept his dark blood-legacy.
- **Secret:** He suffers from severe, violent sleepwalking episodes (parasomnia) that he hides from his fiancee.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes science and medicine can explain every human affliction and that the past is firmly dead.
- **Changes the plot when:** He discovers his own guilt by secretly installing a CCTV camera in his clinic to monitor the intruders, only to watch footage of himself leaving with a sacrificial blade.
- **Arc:** From a pragmatic, rational man of science to a fugitive avatar of divine retribution who sacrifices his normal life for justice.

### Balaram Rout (55) — The antagonist (the man who framed the hero's father)
*Villain. Wears crisp white kurtas, heavy gold rings, always accompanied by armed guards.*

- **Wants:** To win the upcoming election and bulldoze the old Kali temple to build a lucrative tourist resort.
- **Needs:** To be held accountable for the blood on his hands.
- **Secret:** He possesses the original diamond eyes of the Goddess, using them as collateral for political funding.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes power and money make him immune to consequences and that religion is just a tool to control the masses.
- **Changes the plot when:** He frames Soumya for the new murders, planting evidence to recreate the exact mob justice he used on Soumya's father.
- **Arc:** From an untouchable, arrogant ruler of the region to a terrified, broken man executed publicly during the final ritual.

### Kavita (27) — His fiancee
*Ally and Anchor. Modern, practical, wears elegant sarees or doctor's coats, carries high-end medical gear.*

- **Wants:** To bring Soumya back to Bhubaneswar and marry him.
- **Needs:** To realize she cannot save Soumya from his destiny.
- **Secret:** She secretly scheduled Soumya for psychiatric institutionalization because she fears his night-terrors.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes love and therapy can fix any deep-rooted trauma.
- **Changes the plot when:** She inadvertently brings the police to Soumya's location by tracking his phone to save him, escalating the climax.
- **Arc:** From a controlling, protective partner to a horrified witness who must let the man she loves go.

### Inspector Meera (30) — No relation - the investigating police officer
*Skeptic / Detective. Tough, practical police uniform, carries a notebook and a heavy flashlight.*

- **Wants:** To logically solve the bizarre murders and get a promotion to the city.
- **Needs:** To accept that some forces in Rambhadipur defy the Indian Penal Code.
- **Secret:** She was demoted to this village for speaking out against a corrupt politician and hates politicians.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes every crime has a clear, mundane motive and a rational human perpetrator.
- **Changes the plot when:** She uncovers the 15-year-old police file detailing the Pujari's murder, linking the current victims to the old mob.
- **Arc:** From a strict enforcer of the law to someone who looks the other way, allowing the Goddess's justice to be completed.

### Deba (45) — No relation - the wealthy local contractor
*Henchman / Victim. Loud printed shirts, heavy gold chains, constantly chewing paan.*

- **Wants:** To secure the temple demolition contract from Balaram.
- **Needs:** To confess his sins before the Goddess.
- **Secret:** He kept a silver coin from the temple treasury as a twisted lucky charm.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes his wealth has washed away the sins of his past.
- **Changes the plot when:** He dies in the cold open, screaming from phantom burns, setting the entire investigation in motion.
- **Arc:** Dies early, establishing the terrifying stakes and the method of the Goddess's vengeance.

### Sulochana (75) — His father's mother (deceased)
*Mentor (Posthumous). Seen in flashbacks and photos: traditional white widow's saree, deep wrinkles, fearful eyes.*

- **Wants:** To protect Soumya from the village's curse.
- **Needs:** To tell the truth about her son's murder.
- **Secret:** She recognized Deba and Balaram in the mob but stayed silent to keep Soumya alive.
- **Lie they believe:** Believed running away would permanently sever their ties to Kali Mata.
- **Changes the plot when:** Leaves behind audio cassettes disguising the perpetrators' identities as demons in Danda Nata fables, which Soumya decodes.
- **Arc:** Remains a tragic figure, but her voice from the past guides Soumya to the truth.

### Madhab (25) — No relation - local temple caretaker
*False Suspect. Ash smeared on forehead, wears simple dhoti, gaunt and intense.*

- **Wants:** To restore the Kali temple to its former glory.
- **Needs:** To find a true purpose beyond blind fanaticism.
- **Secret:** He has been sneaking into the clinic at night to steal medical supplies for the village poor.
- **Lie they believe:** Believes the Goddess requires human blood sacrifice from the villagers to be appeased.
- **Changes the plot when:** He steals the hero's discarded bloody clothes to hide them, making Inspector Meera suspect him of the murders.
- **Arc:** From a creepy, suspicious fanatic to a loyal devotee who realizes the true avatar is Soumya.

**The villain's plan:** Balaram Rout plans to secure his upcoming re-election by initiating a massive commercial development over the sacred temple lands, turning Rambhadipur into a lucrative tourist hub. He plans to bulldoze the eyeless Kali temple entirely. When the bizarre murders of his old co-conspirators begin, he realizes someone is targeting his past. Believing it to be political sabotage or a surviving relative, his plan is to identify the killer and frame them for the crimes, inciting a village mob to execute them—exactly as he did to the Pujari 15 years ago. He is completely convinced he is right, believing that progress, wealth, and power justify erasing obsolete, superstitious traditions and anyone who clings to them.

## 4. Facts sheet

**Jobs and money:**

- Dr. Soumya earns a lucrative salary at a private city hospital but carries a heavy mortgage, leaving him with little liquid cash.
- Balaram Rout possesses massive untraceable cash reserves hidden in agricultural fronts, allowing him to buy off local authorities.
- Rambhadipur's economy is entirely dependent on Balaram's construction jobs and seasonal farming, making the villagers fearful of crossing him.
- The local clinic is severely underfunded, relying on expired medicines and donations, prompting Soumya to fund it out of pocket.

**Health:**

- Soumya suffers from severe clinical parasomnia (sleepwalking) triggered by extreme stress; his body moves fluidly while his mind is completely unconscious.
- The victims die from bizarre, medically inexplicable trauma mimicking Danda Nata penances, such as internal blistering without external fire, or drowning with dry lungs.
- Sulochana died of a sudden, severe stroke, which acted as the psychological trigger for Soumya's repressed memories.
- Due to his medical training, Soumya has high physical stamina and anatomical knowledge, which the Goddess utilizes to enact precise, lethal justice.

**Belongings and vehicles:**

- Soumya drives a mid-range, heavily financed SUV that stands out awkwardly in the dusty village streets.
- Balaram travels in a convoy of matching white Scorpios, symbolizing his political dominance and intimidation.
- Sulochana left behind a locked wooden box containing old cassette tapes and a rusted brass key to the temple's forgotten back door.
- The local clinic lacks a working ambulance, forcing characters to rely on personal vehicles for emergency transport.

**Places:**

- Rambhadipur is a sweltering, drought-prone village characterized by red dust, intense heat, and narrow, claustrophobic lanes.
- The old Kali temple is half-ruined, overgrown with banyan roots, and houses a terrifying, eyeless idol of the Goddess shrouded in darkness.
- The village clinic is a dusty, poorly lit two-room concrete structure with flickering tube lights and a rusty examination table.
- Soumya's apartment in Bhubaneswar is sleek, sterile, and heavily air-conditioned, representing the safe, modern world he is forced to abandon.

**Rules of this world:**

- The Goddess cannot manifest as a ghost or floating spirit; she strictly requires a willing or traumatized human blood-vessel from the Pujari's lineage to affect the physical world.
- The possession can only occur when the vessel's conscious mind is completely suppressed, which happens during Soumya's deep sleepwalking states.
- Supernatural vengeance adheres strictly to the rules of karma and the specific rituals of the Danda Nata (Dhuli Danda for greed, Pani Danda for deceit), meaning the Goddess cannot just strike someone down randomly.
- Technology functions normally in this world; the supernatural does not disable cameras or phones, which is why Soumya's actions are ultimately captured on CCTV.

## 5. The climax (designed first)

**Where/when:** The final night of the Chaitra month festival during the Meru Jatra, directly in front of the half-ruined Kali temple in Rambhadipur, surrounded by a violent village mob.

**The reversal:** Balaram successfully incites the mob to execute Soumya, perfectly mirroring the Pujari's murder 15 years ago. Instead of trying to escape or reason with them, Soumya realizes the only way to expose Balaram and save the village is to stop fighting his condition and let history repeat itself with a divine twist.

**The hero's choice:** Soumya deliberately injects himself with a heavy psychiatric sedative, intentionally inducing the deep-sleep state required for the Goddess to possess him completely, knowing he will wake up as a murderer.

**The cost:** He permanently breaks his medical oath, becomes a wanted fugitive on the run, loses his modern life and fiancee, and accepts his fate as the eternal, unwilling vessel of Kali Mata.

**Payoffs that fire:**

- Kavita's psychiatric sedative syringe.
- Sulochana's cassette tape fable about the Demon King's silver leg.
- The Danda Nata Jhuna (flammable resin) throwing ritual.
- The battery-operated CCTV camera installed in the clinic.

**Final image:** Soumya, now a fugitive, walking alone on a dark Odia highway at dawn. He turns back toward the camera, and for a split second, his own eyes flash with the brilliant, terrifying light of the restored diamond eyes.

**Why "wow":** The audience realizes the hero's ultimate victory requires him to actively surrender his free will to the Goddess, weaponizing a debilitating medical condition to enact divine justice right in front of the villain who thought he had won.

**After:** Balaram is dead and his 15-year corruption is exposed. Inspector Meera closes the old murder file but must now hunt Soumya. Kavita returns to the city heartbroken. The village's Kali idol finally has its diamond eyes restored by the blood-soaked hands of the Pujari's son.

## 7. Episode blueprints

### Episode 1: The Eyeless Mother

- **Cold open:** The sweltering heat of Chaitra month. Danda Nata dancers perform on burning coals. Performers throw Jhuna resin into the fire, creating massive bursts of flame. Suddenly, wealthy local contractor Deba screams not in ecstasy, but in agony. His skin blisters from the inside out, and he collapses dead beneath the terrifying, eyeless Kali idol.
- **Question:** What invisible force is causing a grotesque, fiery epidemic in the village of Rambhadipur?
- **Hero's move:** Dr. Soumya performs an unauthorized autopsy on Deba in the dusty village clinic, digging into the victim's throat with forceps to extract a blackened, ancient silver coin from the temple treasury.
- **Villain's move:** MLA Balaram Rout immediately orders his men to seize Deba's body and cremate it without a police report, desperate to destroy any evidence connecting the death to the temple.
- **Clue (earned):** Soumya matches the silver coin's insignia to a dusty temple registry he finds abandoned in the clinic's storage room, realizing the victim was stealing.
- **False lead:** The village headman claims Deba contracted a rare, highly contagious skin virus from the city.
- **Hero learns:** The physical trauma on the victim is medically impossible—internal third-degree burns with absolutely no external fire damage.
- **Audience learns:** Balaram is terrified of something from his past, and Soumya is deeply connected to this temple.
- **Set piece:** The chaotic, hypnotic Danda Nata Agni Danda (fire penance) ritual, filled with drums, trance, and sudden, inexplicable death.
- **Side story:** Inspector Meera arrives from the city, instantly suspicious of Balaram's rushed cremation orders.
- **Turn:** Soumya decides to stay in Rambhadipur, using his own money to keep the clinic open to investigate the 'disease' himself.
- **Ending hook:** Soumya washes Deba's blood from his hands at the clinic basin, but as he looks down, the running tap water turns into thick, dark blood.
- **Plants:** Danda Nata performers throwing Jhuna (flammable tree resin) into fire, creating massive, intense bursts of flame.

### Episode 2: Demon of the Red Dirt

- **Cold open:** Soumya drives his heavily financed SUV through the claustrophobic, red-dust lanes of Rambhadipur, listening to his late grandmother's cassette tapes. He blows a tire right outside the overgrown ruins of the Kali temple.
- **Question:** Who is targeting Balaram's inner circle using ancient folk methods?
- **Hero's move:** Soumya decodes his grandmother's taped fable about the 'Demon of the Red Dirt' and cross-references it with local land deeds, actively tracking down Balaram's former surveyor.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram threatens the local Panchayat, demanding emergency authorization to bulldoze the Kali temple by the end of the week to stop the 'curse'.
- **Clue (earned):** Soumya finds the surveyor's hidden ledger detailing illegal temple land transfers, earned by breaking into the surveyor's locked field office.
- **False lead:** None. The surveyor's death looks like an asthma attack brought on by a sudden dust storm.
- **Hero learns:** The surveyor and Deba were both part of a specific land-grab operation fifteen years ago.
- **Audience learns:** The 'Demon' in Sulochana's fairy tale is actually Balaram Rout.
- **Set piece:** The Dhuli Danda (Dust Penance) sequence: The corrupt surveyor is trapped in his own vehicle as it inexplicably fills with choking red temple dust, suffocating him.
- **Side story:** Balaram walks with a prominent silver-headed walking stick, aggressively using it to point and intimidate the villagers.
- **Turn:** Inspector Meera confiscates the ledger from Soumya, warning him not to play detective.
- **Ending hook:** Soumya finds his own clinic boots caked in the exact same red temple dust found in the surveyor's lungs, despite having no memory of going near the temple.
- **Plants:** Sulochana's audio cassette tells a Danda Nata fable about the 'Demon of the Red Dirt' who swallowed two stars and hid them in his silver leg.

### Episode 3: The Midnight Scalpel

- **Cold open:** Soumya wakes up on his clinic cot, exhausted, sweating, and suffering from a severe migraine. He checks his medical kit and finds a surgical scalpel missing and muddy footprints leading out the door.
- **Question:** Who is breaking into the clinic at night and stealing medical supplies?
- **Hero's move:** Refusing to involve the corrupt local police, Soumya purchases and secretly installs a battery-operated CCTV camera above the clinic door to catch the intruder.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram feeds Inspector Meera a fabricated tip that the temple caretaker, Madhab, has a violent grudge against the dead men.
- **Clue (earned):** Soumya finds a scrap of a traditional Odia dhoti snagged on the clinic's rear window frame, matching Madhab's clothing.
- **False lead:** Madhab, the temple caretaker, is seen sneaking around the clinic in the dead of night with a suspicious bag.
- **Hero learns:** Someone with physical access to his clinic is directly involved in the bizarre village deaths.
- **Audience learns:** Soumya's sleepwalking condition (parasomnia) is getting worse; he is losing chunks of time.
- **Set piece:** A tense, silent nighttime stakeout inside the pitch-black clinic, as Soumya waits with a flashlight for the thief to arrive.
- **Side story:** Madhab is secretly stealing expired painkillers to treat poor villagers who Balaram has banned from the hospital.
- **Turn:** Inspector Meera arrests Madhab based on Balaram's tip, closing the official investigation.
- **Ending hook:** While sweeping the clinic floor, Soumya finds a bundle stuffed under his own bed: his own spare shirt, completely soaked in fresh blood.
- **Plants:** A battery-operated CCTV camera Soumya installs in the clinic to catch the intruder stealing medical supplies.

### Episode 4: The Water Penance

- **Cold open:** Flashback to April 2009: A young Soumya cowers under a bed as violent mob noises echo outside. His grandmother Sulochana frantically packs a single bag, her hands covered in someone else's blood.
- **Question:** What is the true connection between Soumya's family and the targeted men?
- **Hero's move:** Soumya uses a rusted brass key from his grandmother's belongings to unlock the forgotten back door of the Kali temple, searching for physical proof of her stories.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram hires outside thugs in white Scorpios to patrol the village, enforcing a strict curfew to protect his remaining allies.
- **Clue (earned):** Inside the temple's hidden sanctum, Soumya uncovers a 15-year-old bloodstained cloth wrapped around an empty jewelry box—the box that held the Goddess's diamond eyes.
- **False lead:** The local drunk claims he saw a vengeful ghost drowning people in the pond.
- **Hero learns:** The Kali idol did not just lose its eyes to decay; they were violently pried out and stolen.
- **Audience learns:** Inspector Meera discovers a heavily redacted 15-year-old case file revealing Soumya's true last name and his identity as the murdered Pujari's son.
- **Set piece:** The Pani Danda (Water Penance): Balaram's former corrupt police chief is found standing frozen in the waist-deep village pond, dead. Autopsy reveals he drowned with completely dry lungs.
- **Side story:** Inspector Meera connects the names of the current victims directly to the mob that murdered the Pujari 15 years ago.
- **Turn:** Meera confronts Soumya at the clinic, slamming the 15-year-old case file on his desk, exposing his hidden identity.
- **Ending hook:** Balaram, informed by a spy that the Pujari's son has returned, smiles coldly and says, 'Time to finish what we started.'

### Episode 5: The Sedative

- **Cold open:** Kavita arrives in Rambhadipur in a sleek, air-conditioned city car. She steps out into the sweltering, dusty village and is horrified to find Soumya looking manic, hollow-eyed, and unwashed.
- **Question:** Can Soumya's modern medical logic explain away his deteriorating mental state?
- **Hero's move:** Desperate for answers about the bloody shirt, Soumya sneaks into the local lockup by bribing a guard, forcing a face-to-face confrontation with the imprisoned Madhab.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram sends his thugs to ransack Soumya's clinic under the guise of a burglary, destroying Soumya's medical notes and equipment.
- **Clue (earned):** Madhab reveals to Soumya that he didn't put the bloody clothes under the bed; he found them draped over the Kali idol and hid them to protect Soumya.
- **False lead:** Kavita insists Soumya's symptoms are just extreme stress-induced psychosis from being back in a traumatizing place.
- **Hero learns:** Madhab is innocent, and whoever is committing the murders is trying to frame Soumya or is intrinsically tied to him.
- **Audience learns:** Kavita secretly planned to have Soumya institutionalized, fearing his violent night-terrors in the city.
- **Set piece:** A chaotic clash at the clinic when Balaram's thugs attack. Soumya uses his anatomical knowledge to brutally incapacitate a thug, showcasing a disturbing physical capability.
- **Side story:** Kavita desperately tries to convince Inspector Meera that Soumya is medically unfit and needs an escort back to Bhubaneswar.
- **Turn:** Soumya refuses to leave, realizing that running away 15 years ago is what allowed Balaram to destroy his family.
- **Ending hook:** During a heated argument, Kavita pulls out a syringe with a powerful psychiatric sedative, begging him to use it. Soumya takes it but refuses to inject it, staring at the needle as night falls.
- **Plants:** Kavita brings a powerful psychiatric sedative in a syringe, begging Soumya to use it so he doesn't sleepwalk and can be safely taken to a city hospital.

### Episode 6: The Footage

- **Cold open:** Inspector Meera examines the corpse of the drowned police chief on a slab. She discovers precise, surgical incisions behind the victim's ears—cuts that only a highly trained doctor could make in the dark.
- **Question:** Who is the invisible assassin executing the Danda Nata penances?
- **Hero's move:** Remembering the camera he installed days ago, Soumya finally removes the SD card from the battery-operated CCTV and plays it on his laptop to identify the killer.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram publicly announces a massive bounty on the killer's head, officially naming 'the deranged city doctor' as the prime suspect to whip the village into a frenzy.
- **Clue (earned):** The video timestamp matches the exact hour of the surveyor's death.
- **False lead:** None. The brutal truth is revealed.
- **Hero learns:** Soumya watches the footage in absolute horror: his own sleeping body rises from the cot, eyes wide but unseeing, grabs a surgical scalpel, and walks out into the night with lethal purpose.
- **Audience learns:** Soumya is the killer. The Goddess is using his severe sleepwalking condition to enact her divine retribution.
- **Set piece:** A tense, psychological sequence of Soumya watching his own terrifying, fluid movements on screen, intercut with flashes of the actual murders he committed while unconscious.
- **Side story:** Meera officially issues a warrant for Soumya's arrest based on the surgical evidence.
- **Turn:** Accepting his horrific guilt, Soumya packs his bags to surrender to Meera and protect the village from himself.
- **Ending hook:** Soumya tries to drive out of Rambhadipur, but a convoy of Balaram's white Scorpios blocks the only highway out, trapping him in the village.
- **Pays off:** Soumya checks the footage expecting to see Madhab, but watches in horror as his own sleeping body takes a surgical scalpel and walks into the night.

### Episode 7: History Repeats

- **Cold open:** Nightfall. Balaram stands on the hood of his Scorpio with a loudspeaker, rallying a mob with torches. He declares Soumya a demon cursing the village—a direct, chilling mirror of the night the Pujari was killed.
- **Question:** Can Soumya survive the mob justice that claimed his father?
- **Hero's move:** Trapped and hunted, Soumya abandons his vehicle and navigates the narrow village lanes to hide inside the overgrown ruins of the Kali temple, piecing together Balaram's final plan.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram orchestrates a ruthless manhunt, ordering his men to plant the surveyor's missing files in Soumya's clinic to legally justify the mob execution.
- **Clue (earned):** Inside the temple, Soumya finds Balaram's discarded election pamphlets from 2009 stuffed into the idol's hollow base, confirming Balaram funded his rise with the stolen diamonds.
- **False lead:** Meera believes Balaram is just trying to protect the village, unaware he orchestrated the original theft.
- **Hero learns:** Balaram didn't just frame his father; he is using the exact same tactic to eliminate Soumya and bulldoze the temple for a resort.
- **Audience learns:** The Goddess has cornered Soumya deliberately, forcing him to make a stand exactly where his father fell.
- **Set piece:** A high-stakes, claustrophobic game of cat-and-mouse through the festival-decorated village alleys, with the sounds of the Meru Jatra drums masking the mob's approach.
- **Side story:** Desperate to save him, Kavita uses a tracking app on her phone to find Soumya's location, inadvertently leading Meera and the armed mob straight to the temple.
- **Turn:** Soumya realizes he cannot outrun the mob or legally expose a man as powerful as Balaram. The system is rigged.
- **Ending hook:** The heavy wooden doors of the temple are smashed open. The mob drags a bloodied Soumya out into the festival square as the Meru Jatra drums reach a deafening crescendo.

### Episode 8: Meru Jatra

- **Cold open:** The final night of Chaitra. Torches blaze in the festival square. Balaram stands victorious before the eyeless Kali idol, watching his men beat Soumya to his knees. History is perfectly repeating itself.
- **Question:** Will Soumya surrender to the mob, or surrender to the Goddess to break the cycle?
- **Hero's move:** Realizing logic and law have failed, Soumya stops fighting. He pulls Kavita's sedative syringe from his pocket and intentionally injects himself, voluntarily inducing the deep-sleep state required for the Goddess to possess him.
- **Villain's move:** Balaram steps forward to deliver the killing blow with his silver-headed walking stick, screaming that tradition is dead.
- **Clue (earned):** Possessed, Soumya catches the walking stick mid-air and snaps it in half.
- **False lead:** The mob thinks Soumya has merely passed out from the beating, dropping their guard.
- **Hero learns:** The ultimate truth of his grandmother's fable: Balaram kept the Goddess's eyes hidden in plain sight.
- **Audience learns:** Soumya accepts the immense cost of vengeance, permanently sacrificing his humanity and modern life to become Kali's vessel.
- **Set piece:** The ultimate Agni Danda. A terrifying, supernatural martial sequence where the unconscious, possessed Soumya moves with divine brutality, completely overpowering Balaram's armed thugs.
- **Side story:** Kavita and Meera arrive just in time to watch Soumya exact impossible, ritualistic justice, realizing the supernatural is horrifyingly real.
- **Turn:** Possessed Soumya retrieves the original stolen diamond eyes from the broken silver walking stick and restores them to the Kali idol.
- **Ending hook:** Cost and Final Image: Wanted for murder and stripped of his old life, Soumya walks away from the village. Final image: A fugitive walking alone on a dark Odia highway at dawn. ## Appendix: the pitch room

**Direction 1: Bishpan: Eyes of the Mother** — A rationalist investigative journalist from Bhubaneswar returns to his disgraced ancestral village to solve a series of bizarre murders linked to a stolen goddess, unaware that he himself is the divine weapon of vengeance.
